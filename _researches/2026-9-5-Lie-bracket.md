We prove that for every smooth vector field $X$ on a closed smooth manifold $M$, there exist smooth vector fields $Y$ and $Z$ such that $[Y,Z]=X.$ Equivalently, the bracket map $\mathfrak{X}(M)\times\mathfrak{X}(M)\to\mathfrak{X}(M)$ is surjective. In particular, when $M$ is nonempty and has positive dimension, the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{X}(M)$ has bracket width one. The result holds in every dimension and does not require $M$ to be orientable.

The main issue is global compatibility: although the equation can be solved by direct integration inside a flow box, cutoff functions introduce unwanted bracket terms. We overcome this with exact relative constructions. A finite transport argument first produces an exterior solution while preserving prescribed data near a compact subset of a transverse level set. We then extend the pair across transverse graph disks. A loop of compactly supported transverse diffeomorphisms, followed by a time shear and an inverse shift, corrects the resulting scalar transition defect in any prescribed positive width. After arranging the canonical pair $(\partial_t,t\partial_t)$ near an equator, two disjoint graph caps complete the global construction.

As a cotangent-bundle consequence, every smooth fiberwise linear function on $T^*M$ is the Poisson bracket of two smooth fiberwise linear functions.
