This expository note studies what can—and cannot—be reconstructed about a vibrating membrane from the motion of a reflected laser spot. Beginning with the geometry of specular reflection, it derives a calibrated linear observation operator for optical slope measurements and separates two distinct inverse problems: reconstructing an unknown initial state and recovering the time dependence of a source with known spatial profile.

The note develops several complementary results:

- an exact eigenspace criterion and Gramian formula for finite-mode initial-state reconstruction;
- a Volterra reconstruction theorem for known-profile sources, with a sharp two-derivative loss in the stated Sobolev scale;
- explicit invisible-mode and compactness obstructions showing the difference between uniqueness and stability;
- an exact Laplace transfer function for a uniformly driven circular membrane observed through an off-centre circular aperture;
- a sharp finite observation-time criterion for uniqueness, proved using the Titchmarsh convolution theorem; and
- an example showing that smooth compactly supported spatial averaging admits no bounded inverse in any finite-order Sobolev data norm.

The scope is deliberately limited to the specified linear observation models. The note is expository and makes no claim of originality.
